# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Anyone who wants to remember someone they love: a grandchild building an ofrenda for their abuela, someone missing a parent, a friend, a pet later on. They use it on a laptop or phone, usually at home in the evening, in a mood that can swing between fond laughter and quiet grief within one conversation. The job: turn scattered memories into an altar that feels like the person, without having to know every tradition up front.

## Product Purpose
Alebrije Altar is an educational project that shows AI working inside a real product. A friendly alebrije guide interviews the user one gentle question at a time, and each answer becomes something on the altar: a memory card, an illustrated object, a traditional element with its meaning. Success is a user who leaves with an altar that feels personal, learned something true about Día de Muertos, and never felt the AI put words in their loved one's mouth.

## Brand Personality
Warm, handmade, curious. The interface feels like painted wood, cut paper and a market stall in late October: textured and patterned where the altar lives, calm and plain where the user is typing. The alebrije is playful when the user is, quiet when they are not. Festive by default; never loud during grief.

## Anti-references
- Halloween: pumpkins, bats, spooky black-and-neon, horror type.
- Coco / Pixar: no characters, logo, song, recognizable designs, no winged jaguar or Xolo guide.
- Sugar-skull face filters or turning people into Catrinas.
- Generic AI chat apps (centered chat bubble column on grey, sparkle icons).
- "Spiritual" kitsch: glowing orbs, messages from beyond, starfields.

## Design Principles
1. The real photo is sacred: central, unaltered, never stylized.
2. The altar is the hero; the chat is the tool that fills it.
3. Follow the user's mood: festive is the default, quiet is always one tap away.
4. Teach the tradition in place: every element explains itself where it sits.
5. Nothing is added without consent: the alebrije suggests, the user places.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and keyboard access; respect prefers-reduced-motion (papel picado sway and reveals turn off). Plain language that translates well to Spanish later. Comfortable tap targets for older relatives.
